# Autumn Budget 2026: policy costings

*19 October 2026 (MOCK). The layout follows HM Treasury's Budget policy costings document. Every figure is invented for a PolicyEngine rehearsal.*

Each note for a costed measure gives a static and a post-behavioural costing; the post-behavioural figure is the one in Table 4.1. The basis of every costing is stated at the start of its note: gross or net of related receipts or spending; cash (when paid) or accrued (when the liability arises); for indirect taxes, duty only or VAT-inclusive; and financial years (April to March). Each measure is costed on its own against the pre-measures forecast unless its note says it is stacked. Measures without a full note are listed at the end with their Table 4.1 figures. A minus sign is a cost to the Exchequer.

## 1. Threshold freeze to 2032

*Income Tax and National Insurance: Maintain the Personal Allowance, basic rate limit, NICs Primary Threshold, Upper Earnings Limit, Secondary Threshold and Class 4 Lower and Upper Profits Limits at their 2026-27 levels for a further year, to 5 April 2032*

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | +3,705 |
| Post-behavioural Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | +3,555 |

**Basis:** gross; cash; not applicable; fiscal years; UK; income tax, employee and employer Class 1 NICs and Class 4 NICs. The static figure is the change in receipts in 2031-32 at the 2031-32 tax base. Without the measure the Personal Allowance, basic rate limit, NICs thresholds and profits limits would rise with September 2030 CPI in April 2031 (Annex A). Income tax liabilities: Personal Allowance leg £2,053m (34.1 million taxpayers × £280 × an average marginal rate of 21.5%), basic rate limit leg £1,242m (6.9 million higher and additional rate taxpayers outside Scotland, and UK-wide on savings, property and dividend income, × £900 × 20%); 90% of the liability is received in 2031-32. Employee Class 1: Primary Threshold leg +£636m (28.4 million employees × £280 × 8%); Upper Earnings Limit leg −£411m (5.8 million employees above the limit pay 2% rather than 8% on £1,180). Employer Class 1: Secondary Threshold leg +£512m (31.0 million employments × £110 × 15%). Class 4: net liability +£5.6m, a quarter of which is received in 2031-32. In Scotland the measure changes the Personal Allowance and NICs thresholds only. The post-behavioural figure allows for a small taxable income response (4%).

## 3. State Pension Top-up Allowance

*Income Tax: Introduce a State Pension Top-up Allowance from 6 April 2027 for individuals who receive the State Pension, equal to the amount by which their State Pension for the year exceeds the Personal Allowance, rounded up to the next £10, withdrawn by £1 for every £1 of adjusted net income above the annual rate of the full new State Pension plus £1,000*

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | −70 | −125 | −140 | −160 | −310 |
| Post-behavioural Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | −70 | −125 | −140 | −160 | −310 |

**Basis:** gross; cash; not applicable; fiscal years; UK; income tax. The allowance equals the individual's State Pension for the tax year (52 × the Annex A weekly rate) less their Personal Allowance, rounded up to the next £10, where positive. It is withdrawn by £1 for every £1 of adjusted net income above the annual rate of the full new State Pension plus £1,000 (£14,062.40 in 2027-28), but not below nil, and is set against income in the same order as the Personal Allowance. It applies to Scottish taxpayers. Beneficiaries rise from 1.45 million in 2027-28 to 1.75 million in 2030-31; average gains rise from £78 to £96. Liabilities by tax year: 2027-28 £113m, 2028-29 £130m, 2029-30 £148m, 2030-31 £168m. 60% of the relief is given in-year through PAYE codes; the rest, mostly for people whose only income is the State Pension and who are taxed through Simple Assessment, arrives the following year. **Stacking.** The 2031-32 figure is costed after measure 1. On its own the measure has 2031-32 liabilities of £187m (1.85 million × £101) and a static cash cost of £180m; after measure 1 holds the Personal Allowance at £12,570, liabilities are £402m (2.3 million × £175) and the cost is £310m. The behavioural response is negligible.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static Exchequer impact (£m) | −490 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Post-behavioural Exchequer impact (£m) | −490 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

**Basis:** gross; cash; not applicable; fiscal years; England and Wales; top-up only, excluding the supplier-funded £150. Spending, in the year the bill credit is made (December 2026 to March 2027). ## 8. Capital Gains Tax higher rate

*Capital Gains Tax: Increase the higher main rate from 24% to 26% for disposals on or after 6 April 2027, including gains on residential property*

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | +70 | +775 | +805 | +835 | +870 |
| Post-behavioural Exchequer impact (£m) | +15 | +690 | −220 | +490 | +510 | +530 |

**Basis:** gross; cash; not applicable; fiscal years; UK; Capital Gains Tax. Tax on a year's gains is mostly paid on 31 January after the tax year ends, so a tax year's liability lands in the next financial year; gains on UK residential property (9% of liabilities) are paid within 60 days of completion, in the same year. **Static.** Gains taxed at the higher rate, net of the annual exempt amount: £38.5bn in 2027-28 growing 4% a year; 2 percentage points on that base. Static liabilities by tax year: 2027-28 £770m, 2028-29 £801m, 2029-30 £833m, 2030-31 £866m, 2031-32 £901m. **Behaviour.** Forestalling brings gains forward into February and March 2027, taxed at 24%, raising receipts in 2026-27 and 2027-28 and lowering them in 2028-29; realisations fall 3% in steady state. The basic rate (18%), Business Asset Disposal Relief and Investors' Relief are unchanged. Gains use the UK basic rate band for all taxpayers, including Scottish taxpayers.
