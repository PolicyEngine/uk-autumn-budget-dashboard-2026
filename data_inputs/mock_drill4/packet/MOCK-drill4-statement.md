# Autumn Budget 2026: summary of the Chancellor's statement

*Monday 19 October 2026 (MOCK). Released at 12:30 BST as the Chancellor rose. Table 4.1, the policy costings with Annex A, the OBR's forecast, the specimen households and the scoring templates follow at 13:40 BST.*

The Chancellor of the Exchequer today presented the Budget. It gives targeted, temporary help with energy and fuel costs, keeps low-income pensioners out of income tax, supports carers and young workers, asks those with the most to contribute more, and rebuilds the public finances.

## The economy and the public finances

- **Growth.** The OBR forecasts growth of 0.9% this year and 1.2% in 2027, picking up to 1.4% in 2028 and 1.5% in 2029.
- **Inflation.** CPI inflation is 3.5% this year, falling to 2.7% in 2027 and 2.2% in 2028, and reaching the 2% target in 2029.
- **Earnings.** Average earnings grow by 4.4% this year and 3.6% in 2027.
- **Fiscal rules.** Before today's decisions the forecast left £14.6 billion of headroom against the stability rule in 2029-30. The decisions raise £0.2 billion in that year, leaving £14.8 billion. Debt falls as a share of GDP by the end of the forecast, which runs to 2031-32.

## Help with the cost of living

**Fuel duty.** The rises due on 1 January and 1 March will not go ahead. Fuel duty stays at 52.95p a litre until 22 February 2027, rises to 54.95p on 23 February 2027 and to 56.95p on 1 October 2027. RPI uprating resumes in April 2028.

**VAT on petrol and diesel.** VAT on road fuel falls from 20% to 15% from 1 December 2026 to 31 May 2027.

**Energy bills.** This winter, households in England and Wales that get the £150 Warm Home Discount because they receive Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, income-related Employment and Support Allowance or income-based Jobseeker's Allowance get a further £125, and those receiving Pension Credit a further £75, paid for by the taxpayer.

**Families.** From April 2027 the Universal Credit child element rises by £12.50 a month for every child, and Child Benefit gets a new Early Years Addition of £10 a week for each child under 1.

**Housing support.** Local Housing Allowance rates in Great Britain are reset to the 30th percentile of local rents in April 2027 and then held at those levels.

## Backing working people, carers and pensioners

**No income tax for pensioners living on the State Pension.** From April 2027 people who receive the State Pension get a State Pension Top-up Allowance covering the part of their State Pension above the Personal Allowance. It is withdrawn pound for pound from pensioners whose income is more than £1,000 above the full new State Pension.

**Carers.** The Carer's Allowance earnings limit rises from £204 to £230 a week from April 2027.

**Young workers.** From April 2027 employers pay no National Insurance on earnings up to £25,000 of employees aged under 23. A Youth Guarantee pays employers £2,000 for each apprentice aged 16 to 21.

## Fair contributions from those with the most

**Capital gains.** From 6 April 2027 the higher rate of Capital Gains Tax rises from 24% to 26%, including on residential property. A new relief will help business owners who reinvest the proceeds of a sale in UK companies.

**Savings income.** From April 2028 the starting rate band for savings falls from £5,000 to £2,000.

**Second homes.** From tomorrow, 20 October 2026, the Stamp Duty Land Tax surcharge on additional homes rises from 5 to 7 percentage points.

**Banks and energy producers.** The bank surcharge rises from 3% to 6% from April 2027, and the Energy Profits Levy is extended by one year.

## Repairing the public finances

**Thresholds.** The Personal Allowance, the higher rate threshold and the main National Insurance thresholds stay at their current levels for one more year, to April 2032.

**Duties.** Alcohol duty rises by RPI plus 2 percentage points in February 2027. Machine Games Duty rises from 20% to 30%. The sugar threshold for the Soft Drinks Industry Levy is lowered.

**Tax gap.** Online marketplaces will collect VAT on their sellers' sales from 2028, and HMRC gets funding for e-invoicing and compliance.

## Growth, security and devolution

The Budget confirms the defence funding from the Defence Investment Plan, introduces relief for pubs, clubs and venues and a supplement for the largest offices in the business rates system, raises the allowance for agricultural and business property relief in inheritance tax, funds the Your First Home equity loan, and sets out the fiscal devolution roadmap for mayors. The new State Pension uprating rule from April 2030, announced on 29 September, is already in the OBR's pre-measures forecast.
