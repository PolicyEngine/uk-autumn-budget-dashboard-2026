# Autumn Budget 2026: summary of the Chancellor's statement

*Released at 12:30 BST as the Chancellor rose. The Budget documents, the OBR's forecast, the specimen households and the scoring templates follow at 13:40 BST.*

The Chancellor of the Exchequer today presented the Budget. It helps families with the cost of living, keeps low-income pensioners out of income tax, asks those with the most to contribute more, and repairs the public finances.

## The economy and the public finances

- **Growth.** The OBR forecasts growth of 0.8% this year and 1.1% in 2027, picking up to 1.5% in 2028 and 1.6% in 2029.
- **Inflation.** CPI inflation is 3.4% this year, falling to 2.9% in 2027 and 2.2% in 2028, and reaching the 2% target in 2029.
- **Earnings.** Average earnings grow by 4.2% this year and 3.6% in 2027.
- **Fiscal rules.** Before today's decisions the forecast left £13.1 billion of headroom against the stability rule in 2029-30. The decisions cost £2.0 billion in that year, leaving £11.1 billion. Debt falls as a share of GDP by the end of the forecast, which now runs to 2031-32.

## Help with the cost of living

**Fuel duty rises phased.** The rises due on 1 January and 1 March will not happen then. Fuel duty stays at 52.95p a litre until the end of March, rises to 55.95p in April and to 57.95p in October 2027. RPI uprating resumes in April 2028.

**More help with energy bills.** Around 5.4 million households in England and Wales that get the Warm Home Discount will get an extra £100 this winter and next winter, paid for by the taxpayer.

**Housing support restored.** Local Housing Allowance rates in Great Britain return to the 30th percentile of local rents from April 2027, and are kept there each year.

## Backing working people and pensioners

**No income tax on the State Pension.** From April 2027 pensioners get a Pensioner Allowance equal to their State Pension where that is higher than the Personal Allowance, so nobody whose only income is the State Pension pays income tax on it. It is withdrawn gradually from pensioners with incomes above £30,000.

**Help for young workers.** From April 2027 employers pay no National Insurance on the earnings of employees aged under 22, up to the upper limit, as they already do for under-21s.

**Support for small employers.** The Employment Allowance rises from £10,500 to £12,000 from April 2027.

## Fair contributions from those with the most

**A lower additional rate threshold.** From April 2027 the 45p rate starts at £100,000 instead of £125,140.

**Capital gains.** From 6 April 2027 the higher rate of Capital Gains Tax rises from 24% to 28%, and the annual exempt amount falls from £3,000 to £2,000.

**Pension salary sacrifice.** From 6 April 2029 the annual pension salary-sacrifice limit exempt from employee and employer National Insurance falls from £2,000 to £1,000. Pension income tax relief continues. The documents include revised national data determinants for this measure and Capital Gains Tax.

**The most valuable homes.** From April 2028 homes in England worth £5 million to £10 million pay a £10,000 surcharge, and homes worth £10 million or more pay £15,000.

**Carried interest.** More of fund managers' carried interest will be taxed, as the multiplier rises from 72.5% to 80%.

## Repairing the public finances

**Thresholds.** The Personal Allowance, the higher rate threshold and the main National Insurance thresholds stay at their current levels for one more year, to April 2032.

**National Insurance for the self-employed.** The Class 4 main rate rises from 6% to 7% from April 2027.

**Other measures.** Insurance Premium Tax rises from 12% to 13% from June 2027, Remote Gaming Duty rises from 40% to 45%, and HMRC gets new funding to close the tax gap.

## Security, growth and devolution

The Budget confirms the defence top-up from the Defence Investment Plan, raises the threshold for Small Business Rate Relief in England, gives mayors a power to levy a charge on overnight visitor stays from April 2028, and funds the Your First Home equity loan for first-time buyers. The new State Pension uprating rule from April 2030, announced on 29 September, is already in the OBR's pre-measures forecast.
