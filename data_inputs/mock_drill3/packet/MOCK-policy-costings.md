# Autumn Budget 2026: policy costings

*October 2026 (MOCK). The layout follows HM Treasury's Budget policy costings document. Every figure is invented for a PolicyEngine rehearsal.*

Each note for a coded measure gives a static and a post-behavioural costing. The post-behavioural figure is the one in Table 4.1. All costings are on a National Accounts basis, with receipts recorded when paid unless the note says otherwise; the basis of each costing (gross or net, duty only or VAT-inclusive, cash or accruals) is stated in its note. Measures are costed in numbered order, so a measure's figures include its interaction with lower-numbered measures. Measures without a full note are listed at the end with their Table 4.1 figures. A minus sign is a cost to the Exchequer.

## 1. Threshold freeze extension

*Income Tax and National Insurance: Maintain the Personal Allowance, basic rate limit, NICs Primary Threshold, Upper Earnings Limit and Class 4 Lower and Upper Profits Limits at their 2026-27 levels for a further year, to 5 April 2032*

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | +2,945 |
| Post-behavioural Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | +2,830 |

**Basis.** Income tax and NICs, cash receipts. The static figure is the change in liabilities at the 2031-32 tax base: Personal Allowance leg £1,916m (33.5 million taxpayers × £260 × an average marginal rate of 22%), basic rate limit leg £1,104m (6.9 million higher and additional rate taxpayers outside Scotland, and UK-wide on savings and dividends, × £800 × 20%). Income tax receipts in 2031-32 are 90% of the liability; the Self Assessment balance arrives in 2032-33, beyond the forecast. Class 1 employee NICs: Primary Threshold leg +£580m (27.9 million employees × £260 × 8%); Upper Earnings Limit leg −£356m (5.6 million employees above the limit pay 2% rather than 8% on £1,060). Class 4: Lower Profits Limit leg +£48m and Upper Profits Limit leg −£34m on a liability basis, a quarter of which is received in 2031-32. Without this measure the thresholds would rise with September 2030 CPI (2.0%) in April 2031 (Annex A). The Secondary Threshold is not affected. In Scotland the measure changes the Personal Allowance and NICs thresholds only, since Scottish rates and bands are set by the Scottish Parliament. The post-behavioural figure allows for a small taxable income response (4%).

## 2. Pensioner Allowance

*Income Tax: Introduce a Pensioner Allowance from 6 April 2027 equal to an individual's State Pension for the year, rounded up to the next £10, where higher than the Personal Allowance, withdrawn by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £30,000*

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | −290 | −620 | −1,020 | −1,480 | −1,855 |
| Post-behavioural Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | −285 | −615 | −1,010 | −1,465 | −1,835 |

**Basis.** Income tax, cash receipts (pensioners' tax is mainly collected in-year through PAYE and Simple Assessment). The allowance equals the individual's State Pension for the tax year (new State Pension, or basic State Pension plus additional State Pension) at the Annex A weekly rate × 52, rounded up to the next £10: £13,040 for a full new State Pension in 2027-28 and £14,830 in 2031-32. It applies where it is higher than the Personal Allowance, and is withdrawn by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £30,000, but not below the Personal Allowance. It does not change the basic rate limit or the £100,000 Personal Allowance taper. Beneficiaries rise from 3.4 million in 2027-28 to 4.6 million in 2031-32 as more people retire on the full new State Pension, and the average gain rises because the State Pension grows while the Personal Allowance is frozen. **Stacking.** The 2031-32 figure is costed after measure 1: on its own the measure would cost £1,640m in 2031-32 (static); after measure 1 holds the Personal Allowance at £12,570, it costs £1,855m. The behavioural response is negligible.

## 3. Warm Home Discount top-up

*Warm Home Discount: A £100 taxpayer-funded top-up to the £150 Warm Home Discount in winter 2026-27 and winter 2027-28 for eligible households in England and Wales*

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static Exchequer impact (£m) | −540 | −550 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Post-behavioural Exchequer impact (£m) | −540 | −550 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

**Basis.** Spending, cash, in the year the bill credit is made. Eligibility follows the Warm Home Discount in England and Wales (Annex A): on the scheme's qualifying date (23 August 2026 for winter 2026-27; 22 August 2027 for winter 2027-28) the bill payer or their partner receives Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, income-related Employment and Support Allowance, income-based Jobseeker's Allowance or Pension Credit, and is named on the electricity bill. About 5.4 million households in 2026-27 and 5.5 million in 2027-28 receive £100, credited to the electricity account between December and March. The top-up is not taxable and is disregarded for all benefits. Static and post-behavioural figures are the same.

## 4. Capital Gains Tax

*Capital Gains Tax: Increase the higher main rate from 24% to 28% and reduce the annual exempt amount from £3,000 to £2,000 for disposals on or after 6 April 2027*

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | +115 | +1,655 | +1,725 | +1,795 | +1,870 |
| Post-behavioural Exchequer impact (£m) | +95 | +1,260 | −285 | +535 | +945 | +980 |

**Basis.** Capital Gains Tax, cash receipts. Tax on a year's gains is mostly paid on 31 January after the tax year ends, so a tax year's liability lands in the next fiscal year; gains on UK residential property (7% of liabilities) are paid within 60 days of the disposal, in the same fiscal year. **Static.** Gains taxed at the higher rate, net of the annual exempt amount: £36.0bn in 2027-28 rising to £42.8bn in 2031-32; 4 percentage points on that base, plus £210m–£230m from the lower annual exempt amount. Static liabilities by tax year: 2027-28 £1,650m, 2028-29 £1,719m, 2029-30 £1,792m, 2030-31 £1,865m, 2031-32 £1,942m. **Behaviour.** Forestalling: £6.0bn of gains are brought forward into November 2026 to March 2027 at an average rate of 23%, raising 2026-27 liabilities by £1,380m, which are mostly paid in January 2028 (fiscal year 2027-28). Those gains are no longer realised later (£4.5bn in 2027-28 and £1.5bn in 2028-29), and realisations fall 8% in steady state, at a marginal rate of 27%. Post-behavioural liabilities by tax year: 2026-27 £1,380m, 2027-28 £-343m, 2028-29 £502m, 2029-30 £943m, 2030-31 £979m, 2031-32 £1,018m. Business Asset Disposal Relief, Investors' Relief and carried interest are unchanged. The basic main rate stays at 18%, and band use follows UK income tax bands for all taxpayers, including Scottish taxpayers.

## 5. Employer NICs for under-22s

*Employer National Insurance: Extend the zero rate for employees under 21 to employees under 22, on earnings up to the Upper Secondary Threshold, from 6 April 2027*

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | −1,395 | −1,445 | −1,495 | −1,545 | −1,600 |
| Post-behavioural Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | −1,145 | −1,040 | −1,015 | −1,020 | −1,040 |

**Basis.** The static figure is **gross** employer Class 1 NICs: about 0.62 million employments of people aged 21, average employer contributions of £2,250 in 2027-28 (15% above the £5,000 Secondary Threshold, up to the £50,270 Upper Secondary Threshold), growing with earnings. The post-behavioural figure is **net**: it allows for part of the saving to pass through to higher pay over time, which raises income tax and employee NICs, and for higher corporation tax on profits. The pass-through is phased, so the net cost is 82% of the gross cost in 2027-28, falling to 65% by 2031-32. Apprentices under 25 already have a zero rate and are unaffected.

## 6. High Value Council Tax Surcharge bands

*High Value Council Tax Surcharge: Replace the £7,500 band for properties in England valued at £5m and above with a £10,000 band for £5m to £10m and a £15,000 band for £10m and above, from 1 April 2028*

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | 0 | +55 | +55 | +55 | +60 |
| Post-behavioural Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | 0 | +45 | +45 | +45 | +45 |

**Basis.** Council tax surcharge receipts, accrued to the financial year (1 April to 31 March). About 13,800 properties in England are valued between £5m and £10m and 2,600 at £10m or more (April 2026 values). The surcharge is the liability of the owner, not the occupier. Values and bands are in April 2026 terms; the charges rise with CPI from 2029-30 (Annex A). The post-behavioural figure allows for valuation challenges and changes in ownership structures (20%).

## 7. Additional rate threshold

*Income Tax: Reduce the additional rate threshold from £125,140 to £100,000 from 6 April 2027*

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | +1,460 | +1,885 | +1,960 | +2,040 | +2,120 |
| Post-behavioural Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | +760 | +980 | +1,020 | +1,060 | +1,100 |

**Basis.** Income tax, cash receipts. The higher rate limit (the taxable income above which the additional rate applies) falls from £125,140 to £100,000. In Scotland the change applies to savings and dividend income only, because Scottish rates and bands on other income are set by the Scottish Parliament. The £100,000 Personal Allowance taper is unchanged. Liabilities are £1,826m in 2027-28, growing by 4% a year; 80% is received in-year through PAYE and 20% the following year through Self Assessment. The post-behavioural figure applies a taxable income elasticity for top-income taxpayers, removing 48% of the static yield.

## 8. Local Housing Allowance

*Local Housing Allowance: Restore rates in Great Britain to the 30th percentile of local rents from April 2027 and each April thereafter*

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | −1,700 | −1,895 | −2,045 | −2,190 | −2,335 |
| Post-behavioural Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | −1,750 | −1,950 | −2,105 | −2,255 | −2,405 |

**Basis.** Spending, cash. Local Housing Allowance rates in Great Britain are reset to the 30th percentile of local rents in April 2027 and every April after that, instead of staying frozen at April 2025 levels (Annex A). For example, the 2-bedroom rate in the Central Greater Manchester Broad Rental Market Area rises to £1,095.00 a month in April 2027. About 1.15 million Universal Credit and Housing Benefit households gain, by £1,480 a year on average in 2027-28. Northern Ireland sets its own rates and is not included. The post-behavioural figure allows for some pass-through into rents.

## 9. Fuel duty

*Fuel Duty: Hold main rates at 52.95p per litre to 31 March 2027, then 55.95p from 1 April 2027 and 57.95p from 1 October 2027, with RPI uprating resuming on 1 April 2028*

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static Exchequer impact (£m) | −395 | −1,280 | −880 | −905 | −935 | −965 |
| Post-behavioural Exchequer impact (£m) | −385 | −1,240 | −855 | −880 | −905 | −935 |

**Basis.** Duty only, cash. VAT on the pump price is scored separately in the VAT forecast and is not included here. The pre-measure baseline is in Annex A. The post-measure path is 52.95ppl to 31 March 2027, 55.95ppl from 1 April 2027, 57.95ppl from 1 October 2027, then 59.75ppl (2028-29), 61.48ppl (2029-30), 63.20ppl (2030-31), 64.97ppl (2031-32) from each April, uprated by RPI. The duty gap is 3.00ppl in January and February 2027, 5.00ppl in March 2027, 3.97ppl from April to September 2027, 1.97ppl from October 2027 to March 2028, then 2.03ppl (2028-29), 2.09ppl (2029-30), 2.15ppl (2030-31), 2.21ppl (2031-32). The tax base is all road fuel, about 43.0bn litres (2026-27), 43.1bn litres (2027-28), 43.3bn litres (2028-29), 43.4bn litres (2029-30), 43.5bn litres (2030-31), 43.6bn litres (2031-32), of which households buy a little under half; the rest is bought by businesses and for freight. The behavioural response uses short- and long-run price elasticities for petrol and diesel.

## 10. Class 4 NICs

*National Insurance: Increase the Class 4 main rate from 6% to 7% from 6 April 2027*

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | +155 | +615 | +635 | +650 | +670 |
| Post-behavioural Exchequer impact (£m) | 0 | +130 | +525 | +540 | +555 | +570 |

**Basis.** Class 4 NICs, cash. The tax base is profits between the Lower and Upper Profits Limits (£12,570 to £50,270), about £61bn in 2027-28 for about 3.1 million self-employed people, growing 3% a year. Class 4 is paid through Self Assessment: about a quarter of a tax year's extra liability is received in that fiscal year and three-quarters in the next. The rate above the Upper Profits Limit stays at 2%. The post-behavioural figure allows for income shifting and incorporation (15%).
