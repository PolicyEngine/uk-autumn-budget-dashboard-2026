Introduction
This document sets out the assumptions and methodologies underlying
costings for tax and annually managed expenditure (AME) policy decisions
announced since Spring Forecast 2026, where those policies have a fiscally
significant impact on the public finances. All of these costings were submitted
to the independent Office for Budget Responsibility (OBR) for their
certification.

Measures that were certified by the OBR as below the negligible threshold of
£10 million per annum in every year of the forecast period are not considered
in this document.

This document presents detailed information on the main data and
assumptions underpinning the costing of policies in the Budget. Each note
sets out a description of the measure, the base, the methodology for the
costing (including relevant adjustments for behavioural responses) and the
main areas of additional uncertainty, beyond those inherent in the OBR’s
forecast. All costings are presented on a National Accounts basis.

The government will publish further information on the costing
methodologies of a selection of the measures covered in this document by
the end of the year. These additional publications will cover the following
measures:

   •   More Timely Payment: Require Income Tax Self Assessment taxpayers
       to pay more of their liabilities in-year from 2029

   •   Distributions: Modernise the distributions framework from 6 April 2028

   •   Modernising Taxation of Foreign Sovereign Investments: Introduce a
       new statutory regime for the direct taxation of foreign sovereigns from
       1 January 2029

   •   Founders' Relief: Introduce a new CGT relief for qualifying founders of
       high-growth, innovative companies from 6 April 2027

   •   Business Asset Disposal Relief (BADR): Reduce the lifetime limit to
       £500,000 from 6 April 2027

The OBR sets out the approach it has taken to scrutiny and certification of the
costings, and highlight areas of particular uncertainty in its Economic and
Fiscal Outlook (EFO).

Annex A sets out the indexation assumptions included in the public finances
forecast baseline, including all pre-announcements.

Support for Energy Bills: Cut VAT on domestic
electricity from 5% to 0% from 1 October 2026 to 1
July 2027
Measure description
This measure reduces the VAT rate on domestic electricity supplies from 5%
to 0%. It applies to all electricity supplies covered by the existing reduced rate,
including domestic consumption, charities, residential accommodation and
supplies to businesses below the de minimis threshold.

This measure is effective from 1 October 2026 until 1 July 2027.

The tax base
The tax base consists of expenditure on domestic electricity supplies eligible
for the reduced rate of VAT, including qualifying household, charity, residential
care and a small amount of business consumption. The tax base is estimated
using ONS consumer expenditure data.

The tax base is grown over the forecast horizon using the OBR’s forecast for
growth in nominal consumer expenditure on electricity.

Costing
The costing is estimated by applying the pre- and post-measure tax regimes
to the tax base described above.

The costing accounts for a behavioural response whereby lower electricity
prices encourage some consumers to shift some of their consumption from
gas and other fuels towards electricity.

Fuel Duty: Extend the temporary 5p-per-litre duty
cut and defer the planned Retail Prices Index (RPI)
increase to 1 July 2027
Measure description
This measure extends the temporary 5 pence per litre (ppl) cut in fuel duty
that was first introduced on 23 March 2022, by 6 months, from 1 January 2027
to 1 July 2027. This follows the announcement in May 2026 to extend the 5ppl
cut in fuel duty until 31 December 2026.

This will apply to rates for heavy oil (diesel and kerosene), unleaded petrol, and
light oil, with a proportionate percentage cut (equivalent to 5ppl from the
main fuel duty rate of 57.95 ppl) to the rates for other fuels and rebated fuels
where practical.

On 1 July 2027, fuel duty rates will increase by 5ppl and will be uprated in line
with the Retail Prices Index (RPI). From 1 April 2028, fuel duty rates will be
uprated annually in line with RPI.

The extension announced in May 2026 took effect on 1 September 2026, with
the further extension taking effect from 1 January 2027.

The tax base
The tax base consists of every litre of taxable fuel that is made available for use
in the UK. Projected volumes are taken from the fuel duty forecasting model.

The tax base is grown over the forecast horizon using the OBR’s economy
forecast determinants for Gross Domestic Product and real consumer
expenditure.

Costing
The costing is estimated by applying the pre- and post-measure tax regimes
to the tax base described above.

The costing accounts for a behavioural response whereby consumers may
change their consumption in response to changes in pump prices.

Mileage Rates: Increase the tax-free and tax-
deductible car and van rates to 55p for the first
10,000 business miles from 6 April 2026 and the
higher mileage rate to 35p from 6 April 2027
Measure description
This measure increases the Approved Mileage Allowance Payment (AMAP)
rates and Simplified Mileage Rate (SMR) for employees, sole traders and
partnerships using their own cars or vans for business travel. The rates have
been increased to 55p per mile for the first 10,000 miles of travel from 6 April
2026 with an additional increase to 35p per mile for over 10,000 miles of travel
from 6 April 2027.

The tax base
The tax base consists of employees, sole traders and partnerships that use cars
or vans for business purposes and incur allowable business motoring
expenses including those subject to the AMAP regime, Relative Motoring
Expenditure, or those who receive Mileage Allowance Relief.

The tax base is estimated using survey evidence on the self-employed
population and their use of business vehicles, HMRC administrative data and
National Transport Survey data to estimate size of population, average
mileage, and reimbursement rate.

The tax base is grown over the forecast horizon using the OBR’s forecast for
growth in employment, the self-employed population, self-employed income
and the Consumer Price Index (CPI).

Costing
The costing is estimated by applying the pre- and post-measure tax regimes
to the tax base described above.

The costing accounts for behavioural responses from both employees and the
self-employed claiming mileage relief. This includes greater awareness of the
regime from employees and the self-employed leading to increased claims for
Mileage Allowance relief. The behavioural adjustment also accounts for some
employers increasing reimbursement rates, whereas some employers may
choose not to increase employee reimbursement, widening the gap between
the amount reimbursed and the approved mileage rate, and therefore
increasing the value of Mileage Allowance Relief claims.

Parental Pay: Establish a Parental Allowance for
fathers, partners and adopters in Great Britain
from April 2029
Measure description
This measure will introduce a new Parental Allowance, broadly modelled on
Maternity Allowance, for fathers, partners and adopters who are not eligible
for Statutory Paternity Pay or Statutory Adoption Pay because they are self-
employed or do not meet the current earnings or continuity-of-employment
conditions.

Eligible self-employed fathers and partners will receive two weeks paid
between £27 and the statutory rate (currently £194.32) depending on National
Insurance Contributions (NICs). Eligible employed or recently unemployed
fathers and partners will receive 2 weeks paid at the lower of the statutory rate
(currently £194.32) or 90% of average weekly earnings. Eligible adopters will
receive up to 39 weeks on the equivalent basis.

This measure will apply in Great Britain only and will be effective from April
2029.

The cost base
The cost base consists of self-employed fathers, partners and adopters, and
parents who are recently employed, recently unemployed or who may have a
broken or irregular pattern of employment. There are an estimated 166,000
fathers and partners and around 750 self-employed adopters who fall into
these categories.

Costing
The costing assumes an 89% take-up rate. The costing uses OBR forecast
determinants for employment growth and projected births to scale the
eligible population and CPI assumptions to uprate the statutory weekly rate.

records/work-history checks.

Carer’s Allowance: Introduce a taper on earnings
above the Weekly Earning Limit within 2030-31
Measure description
This measure replaces the current full withdrawal of Carer’s Allowance when
a claimant earns over the Weekly Earnings Limit, set at 16 times the National
Living Wage, with a tapered withdrawal based on earnings above the limit.
The taper will be set at 100%.

This measure will begin within the 2030-31 financial year, with a precise start
date confirmed in due course.

The cost base
The cost base is DWP’s forecast for caseload and expenditure on Carer’s
Allowance and Universal Credit, as well as OBR projections for increases in the
National Living Wage.

Costing
The costing is calculated by estimating the additional Carer’s Allowance
expenditure from paying tapered awards on earnings above the Weekly
Earnings Limit versus the current non-payment of the benefit. It also factors
in interactions with Universal Credit awards.

The costing accounts for a behavioural response whereby some Carer’s
Allowance claimants increase their earnings or enter work which results in a
reduction in Carer’s Allowance and Universal Credit expenditure. It also
accounts for the take-up of tapered Carer’s Allowance awards amongst newly
eligible individuals who currently earn above the Weekly Earnings Limit.

See the Data Sources supporting document for Budget 2026 for information
on the methodology on estimated beneficiaries of this measure.

Exchequer impact (£m)
                 2026-27 2027-28 2028-29        2029-30     2030-31   2031-32

Exchequer
                   +0m       +0m       +0m        +0m        -40m      -80m
impact


Areas of uncertainty
The main uncertainties in this costing relate to the size of the behavioural
response in relation to labour supply by carers. Additionally, the take-up of
tapered awards amongst newly eligible individuals is uncertain.

The tax base

Business Rates: Increase business rates relief for
pubs, social clubs and live music venues by
20ppts to 35% from 1 April 2027
Measure description
The government is increasing the relief for pubs, social clubs and all but the
very largest live music venues in England by 20ppt, from 15% to 35% from 1

million or above) will not receive this increased rate of relief, but will continue
to benefit from the previously announced 15% relief in 2026-27 and real terms
freeze in 2027-28 and 2028-29, as announced in January 2026. This will provide
ongoing support to qualifying properties, with the level kept under review
alongside the 2029 revaluation.

The tax base
The tax base for the relief consists of the estimated rateable value of all pubs,
social clubs and live music venues, multiplied by the relevant multiplier for
relevant years.

Costing
The static costing is estimated by applying the pre- and post-measure tax
regimes to the tax base described above. The static cost is adjusted to reflect
that business rates are deductible for Corporation Tax for companies and
Income Tax for the self-employed, and that business rates are devolved to
Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland.
