# Autumn Budget 2026: summary of the Chancellor's statement

*Released at 12:30 BST as the Chancellor rose. The Budget documents, the OBR's forecast and the specimen households follow at 13:40 BST.*

The Chancellor of the Exchequer today presented the Budget. It protects households through the winter, backs working people and pensioners, and asks those with the broadest shoulders to pay their fair share.

## The economy and the public finances

- **Growth.** The OBR forecasts growth of 0.9% this year and 1.2% in 2027, as higher energy prices weigh on spending, before picking up to 1.5% from 2028.
- **Inflation.** CPI inflation is 3.3% this year, falling to 2.6% in 2027 and reaching the 2% target in 2029.
- **Earnings.** Average earnings grow by 4.1% this year and 3.5% in 2027.
- **Fiscal rules.** Before today's decisions the forecast left £9.2 billion of headroom against the stability rule in 2029-30. The Budget's decisions are broadly neutral in that year, leaving £9.5 billion. Debt falls as a share of GDP by the end of the forecast.

## Protecting households this winter

**Fuel duty held until September.** The 3p rise due on 1 January and the 2p rise due on 1 March will not go ahead. Main rates stay at 52.95p a litre until 31 August 2027, then rise to 55.95p on 1 September. RPI uprating resumes in April 2028.

**No VAT on household electricity for another year.** The zero rate on domestic electricity in Great Britain, due to end in March, continues until 31 March 2028. Gas stays at the reduced rate.

**An Energy Price Protection Payment.** Around 5.8 million households receiving Universal Credit or Pension Credit will get a one-off £150 in January.

## Backing working people and pensioners

**No income tax on the State Pension.** From April 2027 people over State Pension age get a higher personal allowance of £13,100, so no one whose only income is the full new State Pension pays income tax. It rises with the State Pension each year and tapers away for pensioners with incomes above £20,000.

**Lower employer National Insurance on every job.** From April 2027 the threshold at which employers start paying National Insurance rises from £5,000 to £5,500.

**A fair State Pension for the long term.** From April 2030 the State Pension rises each year by the higher of inflation and 2.5%. Pension Credit continues to rise with earnings, so the poorest pensioners are protected.

## Asking those with the broadest shoulders to contribute

**Capital gains taxed more like income.** From 6 April 2027 the rates of Capital Gains Tax rise from 18% to 20% and from 24% to 28%.

**The council tax surcharge extended to homes worth £1.5 million.** From April 2028 homes in England valued between £1.5 million and £2 million pay a new £1,500 band.

**Banks and energy producers pay more.** The bank surcharge rises from 3% to 5% from April 2027. The Energy Profits Levy rises to 40% from January and runs to March 2031.

**A fairer tax system.** The Budget also doubles the duty on high-stakes gaming machines, gives pubs and music venues 20% business rates relief, adds a supplement for large distribution warehouses, freezes duty on draught beer and cider, and funds new HMRC compliance activity.
