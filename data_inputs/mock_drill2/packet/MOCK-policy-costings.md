# Autumn Budget 2026: policy costings

*October 2026 (MOCK). The layout follows HM Treasury's Budget policy costings document. Every figure is invented for a PolicyEngine rehearsal.*

Each note gives a static and a post-behavioural costing where the measure is large or novel. The post-behavioural figure is the one in Table 4.1. All costings are on a National Accounts basis. Measures without a full note are listed at the end with their Table 4.1 figures.

## 1. Fuel Duty: Cancel the 3p increase due on 1 January 2027 and the 2p increase due on 1 March 2027, maintaining main rates at 52.95p per litre until 31 August 2027, rising to 55.95p on 1 September 2027, with RPI uprating from 1 April 2028

### Static costing

**Static Exchequer impact (£m)**

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exchequer impact | −395m | −2,245m | −1,745m | −1,775m | −1,800m |

### Post-behavioural costing

**Exchequer impact (£m)**

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exchequer impact | −385m | −2,190m | −1,705m | −1,740m | −1,770m |

The tax base is around 43 billion litres of road fuel a year. The pre-measure baseline is in Annex A. The post-measure path is 52.95ppl to 31 August 2027, 55.95ppl from 1 September 2027 to 31 March 2028, then 57.68ppl, 59.35ppl and 61.01ppl from each April, uprated by RPI. The duty gap is 3.00ppl in January and February 2027, 5.00ppl in March 2027, 6.97ppl from April to August 2027 and 3.97ppl from September 2027 to March 2028. Figures are duty only; VAT effects on the pump price are scored separately within the VAT forecast and are not included here. The behavioural response uses separate short- and long-run elasticities for petrol and diesel.

## 4. Income Tax: Introduce a State Pension Personal Allowance of £13,100 from 6 April 2027 for individuals over State Pension age, tapered by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £20,000 to no lower than the Personal Allowance, and uprated with the full new State Pension from April 2028

### Static costing

**Static Exchequer impact (£m)**

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exchequer impact | 0m | −560m | −715m | −850m | −985m |

### Post-behavioural costing

**Exchequer impact (£m)**

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exchequer impact | 0m | −540m | −690m | −820m | −950m |

The tax base is individuals over State Pension age with income above £12,570, around 7.4 million in 2027-28. The State Pension Personal Allowance replaces the Personal Allowance where it is higher. It is set at £13,100 in 2027-28 and then rises each April by the same percentage as the full new State Pension, rounded up to the next £10. The taper reduces it by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £20,000 until it reaches £12,570. It does not change the basic rate limit. The behavioural response is negligible.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exchequer impact | +450m | +250m | +1,150m | +1,250m | +1,320m |

The tax base is chargeable gains, projected from Self Assessment data. The static costing applies the new rates to the projected gains. The behavioural response includes forestalling (gains brought forward into 2026-27) and lower realisations in later years, using an elasticity of realisations to the marginal rate. Receipts arrive mainly in the January after the tax year. The annual exempt amount stays at £3,000. Business Asset Disposal Relief and Investors' Relief are unchanged.

## 8. State Pension: From April 2030, uprate the basic and new State Pension by the higher of CPI and 2.5%

### Static costing

**Static Exchequer impact (£m)**

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exchequer impact | 0m | 0m | 0m | 0m | +1,660m |

### Post-behavioural costing

**Exchequer impact (£m)**

| | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exchequer impact | 0m | 0m | 0m | 0m | +1,150m |

The measure removes the earnings link from April 2030: both State Pensions then rise by the higher of September CPI and 2.5%. In the OBR's forecast earnings growth in May to July 2029 is 3.4% and CPI in September 2029 is 2.0%, so the April 2030 uprating is 2.5% rather than 3.4%. The Pension Credit standard minimum guarantee continues to rise at least in line with earnings. The post-behavioural figure is net of higher Pension Credit spending and lower income tax receipts.
