# Autumn Budget 2026: candidate reforms, updated 16 October 2026

This update replaces the 8 October survey (`docs/autumn-budget-2026-candidate-reforms.md` in `uk-autumn-budget-dashboard-2026`). It is **real pre-Budget research, not mock data**. Nothing here is announced policy unless it is marked **announced** or **committed**.

The Budget and the OBR forecast are on Wednesday 28 October 2026 ([Chancellor's letter, 31 Jul](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/chancellor-letter-to-the-treasury-select-committee-tsc-budget-2026-date/chancellor-letter-to-the-treasury-select-committee-tsc)). It is John Healey's first Budget as Chancellor under Prime Minister Andy Burnham. Copy must not say "Reeves' Budget".

## 1. How to read this

**Source quality.** The FT, Times, Telegraph, Guardian, BBC, Independent, Mail and Bloomberg were not fetched directly. Their stories are cited through syndicated copies (Yahoo, AOL, investing.com, City AM, NewsBeep, Ground News) and marked "via". Bloomberg pages return 403, so Bloomberg rows rest on headlines or syndication. Pages were read through a summarising tool, so quotes are near-verbatim; check any figure against the original before publication. The IFS Green Budget is the exception: its PDF was read in full. **Unverified** marks a claim with no primary source.

**Likelihood scale.** High, medium-high, medium, medium-low, low. Ranks within a level put **announced** and **committed** items first, then items with the strongest press sourcing.

**Engine baseline.** The dashboard's `main` runs `policyengine` 6.5.0, which pins `policyengine-uk` 2.130.1 and certifies `enhanced_frs_2024_25` (uk-data 1.58.1, sha `056e80fd…`). PyPI has `policyengine-uk` 2.134.2, but 6.5.0 cannot pair with it. The Microcosm national release of 15 Oct (sha `04a496f2…`, built under UK 2.123.0) is not certified by any bundle. Engine statements below are for 2.130.1 unless they say otherwise. Dataset columns were checked on the cached uk-data 1.58.0 file (sha `5ba399ea…`, built with the same policyengine-uk version, 2.122.2, as 1.58.1); the 1.58.1 file is not cached locally.

**Routes.** `main`'s publication gate refuses any model and data pair the bundle has not certified. Each measure gets exactly one primary route for Budget day:

- `dashboard-param`: `parameter_changes` on existing pe-uk parameters, in `src/uk_budget_data/reforms.py`.
- `dashboard-structural`: a `simulation_modifier` in the dashboard repo, because pe-uk lacks the variable.
- `pe-uk-param` / `pe-uk-variable`: a new or fixed parameter, or a new variable or formula, upstream.
- `data`: the bottleneck is a missing or weak dataset input.
- `ledger`: business-side or outside PolicyEngine's household scope; a coverage-ledger row, not a household score.
- `not-computable`: cannot be scored in PE, even with a modifier.

## 2. What changed since 8 October

1. **Fuel duty rises expected to be dropped: medium-high → high.** Healey is "expected to drop plans to increase fuel duty by 3 pence a litre in January and 2 pence in March" (FT via [Reuters/investing.com, 14 Oct](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/uk-finance-minister-healey-set-to-pay-for-budget-measures-without-more-borrowing-ft-reports-4948054)); keeping the freeze was already under consideration ([Bloomberg via Yahoo, 11 Oct](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/healey-plans-short-term-uk-060000168.html)). The duration is not reported. The RF argues the other way, for small predictable rises ([Independent via Yahoo, 13 Oct](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/healey-warned-against-delaying-tough-112011526.html)).
2. **HVCTS cut to £1.5m reportedly rejected: medium-high → low.** Healey "has ruled out" it (Sunday Times and FT via [City AM, 11 Oct](https://www.cityam.com/healey-rules-out-expanding-mansion-tax-to-1-5m-homes/); [Property Week, 12 Oct](https://www.propertyweek.com/news/healey-rules-out-plans-to-lower-mansion-tax-threshold-to-1-5m)). It was rank 4 on 8 Oct and leaves the top 20.
3. **CGT alignment with income tax ruled out; a modest rise stays open; a reinvestment relief is new.** FT via [Reuters/Yahoo, 13 Oct](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/uk-not-raise-capital-gains-094813503.html) and [City AM, 13 Oct](https://www.cityam.com/healey-rejects-calls-to-equalise-capital-gains-and-income-tax-rates/): alignment off; "a more modest increase… has not been ruled out"; the Treasury is "reportedly developing" a relief for entrepreneurs who reinvest sale proceeds in the UK.
4. **Bank surcharge: medium → high.** A bank tax rise is "one of the major measures that Healey submitted to the OBR" (Bloomberg, [headline, 15 Oct](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-15/healey-plans-to-raise-uk-taxes-on-banks-at-budget-this-month), via [Ground News](https://ground.news/article/john-healey-to-raise-taxes-on-banks-in-budget-report_26317c)); the industry expects 3% to return to 8% (Guardian via [NewsBeep, 15 Oct](https://www.newsbeep.com/771823/)). Counter-signal: Healey gave "no steer" and two lender chief executives told Reuters they expect no rise ([Reuters via Yahoo, 15 Oct](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/asked-bank-tax-uks-healey-095132405.html)).
5. **Energy support: first on-record signal.** The PM: "there is more to come, and we will set that out soon" ([Hansard, 12 Oct](https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/2026-10-12/debates/54FB2EDB-83AF-40FE-8A98-A914AD221CD7/InternationalUpdate)). Bloomberg (11 Oct) and the FT (14 Oct) point to the Warm Home Discount as the vehicle. Healey says support will be "targeted" and "temporary" (ITV, per [City AM, 15 Oct](https://www.cityam.com/healey-promises-budget-will-meet-the-moment/)).
6. **Electricity 0% VAT extension past March 2027: medium-low → low.** Healey is "unlikely to recommit"; the decision is "likely to be pushed to next year's spending review" (i via [PressBee, 15 Oct](https://eng.pressbee.net/show4927106.html)).
7. **The package is to be "scorecard positive" in 2029-30.** Measures are paid for by taxes or spending cuts, and headroom is held as a "contingency fund" (FT via [investing.com, 14 Oct](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/uk-finance-minister-healey-set-to-pay-for-budget-measures-without-more-borrowing-ft-reports-4948054)). Expect raisers sized to match the giveaways.
8. **New revenue raisers.** Online marketplaces to collect sellers' VAT, up to £1bn (Times and City AM via [AOL, 14 Oct](https://www.aol.co.uk/articles/healey-plans-1bn-vat-crackdown-082622000.html)); a higher business rates multiplier on large offices, about £1bn (Telegraph via [City AM, 15 Oct](https://www.cityam.com/healeys-1bn-london-office-raid-would-be-attack-on-growth/)). The warehouse surcharge is ruled out (Telegraph via [AOL, 10 Oct](https://www.aol.com/articles/healey-rules-warehouse-tax-raid-103546000.html)). A temporary VAT cut on petrol is floated (Bloomberg, 11 Oct).
9. **Employer NI relief for young hires: low → medium-low** ([City AM, 14 Oct](https://www.cityam.com/exclusive-healey-looks-to-lower-business-costs-of-hiring-neets/)).
10. **IFS Green Budget (published 12 Oct; event 14 Oct)** ([PDF](https://ifs.org.uk/system/files/2026-10/IFS_Green_Budget_2026_0.pdf)). Headroom of about £20bn in an illustrative central case, with a £13–38bn range driven by the OBR's earnings judgement. A £20bn spending top-up is needed in 2030-31, rising to £35bn with pay and productivity pressures; that is a funding need, not a fiscal-rule gap. "History clearly suggests that the fuel duty freeze is likely to be extended." A permanent electricity VAT cut costs about £1.5bn a year. "A 'steady-as-she-goes' Budget is not an option" (Helen Miller).
11. **RF "Hitting the buffers" (13 Oct)** ([press release](https://www.resolutionfoundation.org/press-releases/the-chancellor-must-balance-support-for-struggling-families-at-the-budget-with-the-need-to-rebuild-confidence-in-the-public-finances-as-headroom-halved-to-12-billion/)). Headroom "as little as £12 billion"; rebuild to at least £20bn with about £8bn of tax rises. Its menu sums to £12bn: a 2p employee NI to income tax switch (£6bn), CGT at death and on emigration (£4bn), NI on partnership income (£2bn). Also: LHA relink (£2.3bn), and energy help for households under £24k (£175 average, £2bn). The "RF says £5bn" line in some aggregators has no RF source.

**Rule implied by the certification gate.** On Budget day every household score goes through the dashboard repo: `parameter_changes` where 2.130.1 has the parameter, `simulation_modifier` where it does not. A pe-uk change reaches a Budget-day publication only if it is merged, released to PyPI and picked up by a new certified `policyengine` bundle that the dashboard adopts before the freeze. With 12 days to go and 6.5.0 already unable to pair with 2.134.2, no top-20 measure should depend on that. Upstream work is for permanence after the day: each `dashboard-structural` modifier gets a pe-uk PR (WHD variable, pensioner allowance switch, employer NI age bands, fuel VAT parameter) and replaces the modifier once a certified bundle carries it.

**Data work, ranked by how many top-20 measures it blocks or weakens.**

1. **Capital gains imputation and calibration** (2: ranks 13, 18; plus the watch-list CGT-at-death item). `capital_gains` is imputed on cloned households; there is no base cost, holding period or disposal type; the default forward path is not the OBR's. Calibrate to HMRC CGT receipts by band, and choose the projection scenario (2.130.0) deliberately.
2. **Road-fuel litres** (2: ranks 7, 20). `petrol_spending` and `diesel_spending` are LCFS imputations; implied litres sit below the ONS benchmark (#2169 part B). Calibrate litres to HMRC fuel duty receipts.
3. **Warm Home Discount eligibility and receipt** (1: rank 8). No WHD receipt variable; "named on the bill" and Scottish rules absent; eligibility depends on simulated benefit take-up. Calibrate the count to DESNZ's "around 6 million".
4. **Hiring and age-band employment status** (1: rank 17). No new-hire, NEET or apprentice flags.
5. **Estates and inheritance** (1: rank 19). No IHT model or estate data.
