Drill reruns: drill day vs policyengine 6.10.2 MOCK

The four mock-Budget drills, rerun on 24 October 2026 on today's engine and the certified Microcosm data. Rehearsal measures, not a real Budget.

How to read this

  • A drill was a timed rehearsal of Budget day: we scored a set of invented ("mock") measures as if they had just been announced.
  • Drill-day estimate is what we published on the day, on that day's engine and data. Rerun today is the same measure code run again on policyengine 6.10.2 and the certified Microcosm data.
  • Official costing (static) is the mock Treasury/OBR costing in each drill's packet, before behavioural effects, as recorded in the drill's filled scoring sheet. Drill 1 has none.

Signs and colours

−1.20 a negative £bn figure is a cost to the Exchequer; a positive one raises money.
+0.22 change = rerun minus drill day, in £bn, then as a % of the drill-day figure.
green the rerun is closer to the official static costing.
amber the rerun is further from it.
grey no like-for-like costing (none, different basis, or not a valid rerun).
Fuel duty in drills 1–3: not valid rerunsThose drills set fuel duty month by month. pe-uk 2.132.6 ignores month-dated rates, so only the VAT part of each measure is scored now (drills 2 and 3 fall by 82.9% in every year). Drill 4 used open-ended dates and is unaffected. See the README finding.
Decile 1 moves on its ownpe-uk 2.132.4 resets negative household income to zero and now counts those households in the bottom decile instead of leaving them out. Decile 1 figures from before and after that version are not comparable, even where revenue is unchanged.
Different basis: no colourOur figures are tax-year liabilities (some time-limited measures by calendar year); the costings are fiscal-year cash.
